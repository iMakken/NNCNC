NNCNC is a bike computer, not a measuring instrument. It records your rides with your phone's GPS and keeps them on the phone. By installing the app you agree to what follows — and the short version is that the numbers are good estimates, not facts, and the bike is still your responsibility.

1. What this app is

NNCNC records a bike ride using your phone's satellite positioning and shows speed, distance, time, average and maximum speed, elevation gain and an estimate of calories. It keeps a history of those rides, an odometer per bike, and service intervals you set yourself.

It is a companion for riding. It is not a medical device, not a training or coaching tool, and not a certified speed or distance measuring instrument.

2. The numbers are estimates

Every figure the app shows is derived from consumer GPS, which is accurate to several metres horizontally and worse than that vertically. Buildings, trees, tunnels and weather all degrade it. Distance, speed and especially elevation gain can therefore differ from the true values, and from what another device records on the same ride.

Calories are estimated from distance, time and the body weight you enter, using a standard metabolic equivalent table. Without a heart rate sensor no app can do better than an estimate, and this one does not pretend otherwise — that is why the figure is shown with a tilde. The effort score is a rough characterisation of a ride, not a fitness measurement.

Do not rely on these numbers where accuracy matters: for competition, for a record, for a medical or training decision, or for anything with a legal or financial consequence.

3. Riding safely is on you

Start the recording before you set off and stop it when you arrive. Do not operate the phone while you are riding. Mount it so it cannot fall, and keep your attention on the road rather than on the screen.

You remain responsible for obeying the traffic laws where you ride, for your own safety and for the safety of everyone around you. Nothing the app displays is an instruction to ride faster, further or anywhere in particular.

4. Service reminders are a counter, not an inspection

You choose the service intervals yourself, and the app counts the kilometres it has recorded since you last reset them. That is all it does. It does not examine your bike, it cannot see wear, damage or a part that has failed early, and it knows nothing about rides you made without the app running.

A reminder is a prompt to look at the bike, not a verdict on its condition — and the absence of a reminder is not a statement that anything is fine. Inspecting and maintaining your bike, or having a mechanic do it, remains entirely your responsibility.

5. Background recording and your phone

Recording continues with the screen off, which the app needs location permission to do. Continuous GPS use consumes battery noticeably faster than normal.

Phone operating systems may suspend or shut down a background app to save power, and some manufacturers do this far more aggressively than others. A ride can therefore be cut short or lost through no fault of the app. If your phone has battery optimisation settings, exempting NNCNC from them makes this much less likely.

6. Your data

There are no accounts and no server. Your rides, bikes and settings live on your device and nowhere else, and we never receive a copy.

The consequence is yours to manage: if you delete the app, lose the phone, or reset it, the data goes with it. Use export data to write everything to a file you keep, and import data to bring it back. The Privacy Policy explains the rest.
